One rezoning. Two decades of drift.

Choice and magnet programs have been pulling students out of their zoned schools for years, leaving some campuses over capacity and others hollowed out. In March 2026, Alachua County redrew its attendance lines for the first time since 2003. This is the imbalance the map inherited — and the question of whether it was actually fixed.

I. The exodus

Choice programs aren't drawing the same district they sit in

Magnet and IB seats are open to any student in the county, and thousands apply every cycle. But the students who get in look different from the district as a whole — and every seat filled by a transfer is a seat, and a per-pupil dollar, that leaves a zoned neighborhood school.

District staff told the school board magnet enrollment is "not mirroring our district demographics in a number of ways in all of our racial groups" — while the data below (from that same presentation) shows exactly how far off it runs. — Paraphrased from district staff remarks, ACPS Magnet Board Workshop, November 8, 2023, as reported by Alachua Chronicle

II. The consequence

A district losing students, class period by class period

Districtwide enrollment peaked in 2018-19 and has fallen since — even after COVID's dip passed, ACPS never recovered to its old size, and 2024-25 is now the lowest enrollment point in over a decade. Fewer students districtwide means less total per-pupil funding flowing in — and less funding makes it harder to recruit and keep every classroom staffed by a teacher certified in what they teach.
