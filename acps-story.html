Redrawing the Lines
An enrollment investigation · Alachua County Public Schools

One rezoning. Two decades of drift.

Choice and magnet programs have been pulling students out of their zoned schools for years, leaving some campuses over capacity and others hollowed out. In March 2026, Alachua County redrew its attendance lines for the first time since 2003. This is the imbalance the map inherited — and the question of whether it was actually fixed.

I. The exodus

Choice programs aren't drawing the same district they sit in

Magnet and IB seats are open to any student in the county, and thousands apply every cycle. But the students who get in look different from the district as a whole — and every seat filled by a transfer is a seat, and a per-pupil dollar, that leaves a zoned neighborhood school.

"We have to be honest that our choice programs are not yet reflecting the diversity of our overall student population." — Presented to the Alachua County School Board, November 2023

II. The consequence

Left behind, one class period at a time

Lake Forest Elementary sits inside a zone that's lost enrollment for years. Fewer students means less per-pupil funding flowing to the school — and less funding makes it harder to recruit and keep teachers certified in what they teach. Two zoned schools elsewhere in the county have swung the other way, now enrolling more students than their buildings were designed to hold.

Reading these charts

Each grey dot is one ACPS school. The red dot and line trace Lake Forest Elementary specifically. Where dots trend upward together, the relationship holds; where they scatter, it breaks down — which is exactly where the rezoning debate lives.

Underlying school-level figures here are placeholders standing in for a full FL DOE data pull; Lake Forest's own numbers (209 students, 8.6% out-of-field) are the reported figures above.
